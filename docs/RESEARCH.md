### Math, kindergarten – 2nd grade: NEW curriculum (read 2026-10-29)

⚠️ The 2006 grade documents in the table above are **superseded for grades 1–3**: the new curriculum applies to
grade 1 from 2024‑25, grade 2 from 2025‑26 and grade 3 from 2026‑27 (2025 letter p.2; 2026 letter p.2). Use these:
grade 1 `https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Mazkirut_Pedagogit/math/primary-school/math2023/Newprogramgrade1.pdf`,
grade 2 `https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Mazkirut_Pedagogit/math/primary-school/math2023/Newprogramgrade2.pdf`,
kindergarten (2010) `https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Mazkirut_Pedagogit/Matematika/TochnitKdamYesodiHeb.pdf`.

- **Gan (pp.12–14):** count aloud 1–30 and back from 10; count objects to 20; compare groups by matching and counting;
  ordinals to 6th; estimate; symbols 0–10 (ext. 20), zero after the others; join/separate within 10 with objects;
  equal sharing to 10; "half" only as everyday language; dot dice; patterns, shapes, informal measuring, coins.
- **Grade 1:** numbers to 100 and 0 (~45h), operations within 100 (~45h), shapes, length, time (whole hours), data.
  Count forward/back to 100, by 10s/5s/2s; subitizing; grouping into 10s; read/write numbers incl. digit direction;
  number line (exact and approximate placement, misplaced number); between/before/after/closer to.
  Add/subtract within 10 then 20, any strategy; **firm command of all splits of 10**; = as "same amount"; missing
  numbers on either side; whole tens to 100 by analogy; money to 20 ₪ with change.
  **Not in grade 1:** ×/÷, odd/even, < > signs (all moved later).
- **Grade 2:** numbers to 1,000, place value and flexible splits, < >; counting by 10/20/50/100; odd/even (0 is even);
  **full command of facts within 20**; two-digit add/subtract in a row then columns; estimating, error finding;
  comparison word problems; multiplication as repeated addition/arrays — **2, 4, 5, 10 tables + division** (sharing
  and grouping); cm/m, perimeter; solids; clock to the half hour. **Not in grade 2:** parentheses, divisibility,
  fractions ½ ¼, area, weight.
- **Representations named:** number line, hundred chart, abacus, place-value "house", bundled sticks, coins, arrays,
  dot dice, digital manipulatives. Ten-frame and base-ten blocks are *not* named. Any reasonable strategy is accepted.
- **Item formats:** "who am I?" clues; missing numbers on a line or chart; misplaced number; missing addend on either
  side; build an exercise for a target; compare without solving; odd one out; open problems with several answers; money change.
- **Fluency** is framed as command/automatic recall, never speed — consistent with our no-timer rule.
- **Follow-up for the clock island:** the new grade 2 teaches the clock to the *half* hour; our `clock.quarter` skill is
  tagged grade 2 from the old curriculum — re-check its grade against the new grade 3 document.

### Math, grades 3–6: NEW curriculum (read 2026-11-10)

Sources (all four fetched; PDFs, text extracted with PDFKit):
`https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Mazkirut_Pedagogit/math/primary-school/math2023/Newprogramgrade3.pdf` (19 pp.),
`…Newprogramgrade4.pdf` (23 pp.), `…Newprogramgrade5.pdf` (36 pp.), `…Newprogramgrade6.pdf` (24 pp.).
Citations are `[G3 p.N]` and so on. Page N is the PDF page; the grade 4 and 5 documents print the same numbers.
Each document is a three-column table: topic and sub-items, didactic notes and examples, and links to other topics.

**Changes from the 2006 summary above (the parts that matter for planning):**
- Grade 3 has **no fractions**. Remainder notation is the only groundwork for them [G3 p.9].
- Fractions start formally in grade 4. Decimals and primes come in grade 5. Decimal ×/÷, percent and the mean come in grade 6.
- **Ratio and scale do not appear** in the new grade 6 document. Negative numbers and powers (other than powers of 10) do not appear either.
- The documents never use the phrase "by heart" for the times table. Grade 3 says "completing and consolidating the 10×10 table" [G3 p.7] and repeatedly asks for mental calculation (חישובים בעל פה) [G3 p.9, p.13].
- No nikud or reading-level guidance appears in any of the four math documents. Per D23, the app keeps full nikud anyway.
- Every topic asks for the same task types: equations and inequalities with the relation sign on the **left** (`___ = 4 + 5`), more than one solution, error-finding, estimating before solving, and inventing a word problem for an exercise.

#### Where grade 2 (built) ends: grade 3 must not repeat it

`docs/specs/math-grade2.md` skills 11–17 already cover:
- numbers to 1,000 and place value (house, flexible splits, `<` `>`)
- counting by 10/20/50/100 to 1,000, and the hundred chart
- two-digit +/− within 100, with estimating
- facts within 20 (Leitner)
- odd/even (0 is even)
- **×/÷ facts of the 2, 4, 5, 10 tables only**, with no remainders

The clock island's `clock.minute` (grade 3) already reads and sets an analog clock to the minute.

So grade 3 starts with:
- numbers to 10,000, counting by 20/**25**/50/**200/500**
- the **3, 6, 7, 8, 9 tables**, then the full 10×10 table
- division with remainder
- +/− to 10,000

The remaining grade 3 time items are new: digital 12/24-hour clocks and durations.

#### Grade 3 (in schools from 2026‑27)

**1. Topics in document order**

| # | Topic (hours) | Items and ranges | Cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Natural numbers to 10,000 (~25 h) | Place value (ones/tens/hundreds/thousands); read and write in words and digits; count forward/back from any number; count by 1s, 2s, 10s, 100s, 1,000s; **skip counting by 20, 25, 50, 200, 500**; order and compare with `= > <`; **×10, ×100 and ÷10, ÷100** (whole quotients only). Flexible splits: 2,356 = 23 hundreds + 56 ones = 234 tens + 16 ones | p.1–3 |
| 1b | Sequences | Arithmetic sequences with steps of 1/10/100/1,000, 50/200/250; sequences of multiples; find the error; picture sequences; non-uniform jumps | p.4 |
| 2 | Operations to 10,000 (~75 h): +/− | Horizontal and **vertical (column)**; whole tens/hundreds/thousands, also when one addend isn't whole; equations and inequalities; estimation and the `≈` sign; inverse operations; commutative and associative laws (informal, no law names). Subtraction with 0 as subtrahend or difference, but **not 0 as minuend** at this stage | p.5–6 |
| 2b | ×/÷ | **Completing and consolidating the 10×10 table**; × and ÷ as inverses; properties of 0 and 1; commutative and associative laws. Division in two senses (sharing and grouping), without naming them. **No division by 0 in grade 3** (it comes in grade 4) | p.7–8 |
| 2c | Divisibility | Divisibility by **2, 5, 10**, learned through investigation, not as a procedure | p.9 |
| 2d | Division with remainder | **Within the 10×10 table, single-digit divisor.** Written as `13 : 4 = 3 + (1 : 4)` or in words "3 and 1 left over out of 4". **Not** `3(1)` | p.9–10 |
| 2e | Order of operations | Order of operations; parentheses; distributive law where one factor is single-digit (models: cube towers, discs, area rectangles) | p.10–11 |
| 2f | ×/÷ by 10, 100, 1,000 | ×10/100/1,000; × whole tens and hundreds (20 × 300); ÷ where the divisor or quotient is 10/100/1,000; whole tens/hundreds ÷ a single digit; 2-digit ÷ 1-digit by splitting the dividend (no remainder). **Avoid the "just add a 0" rule** | p.11–12 |
| 2g | Rounding | To tens, hundreds and thousands; ≥ 5 rounds up; `≈`; on number lines | p.13 |
| 3 | Angles and triangles (~10 h) | Angle as two arms from a vertex (no formal definition); compare directly or through a third object; **straight, obtuse, right and acute** (by comparing to a right angle, no protractor, nothing > 180°); angles in polygons; name triangles by angles; **classify triangles by both sides and angles** | p.13–15 |
| 4 | Area (~10 h) | Direct comparison; cut and recombine; arbitrary units and grids; **cm² and m²**; **rectangle area with whole sides**, counting squares until it becomes length × width; draw rectangles of a given area on a grid | p.15–18 |
| 5 | Boxes and cubes (~5 h) | Nets of a box; cube vs. a box that isn't a cube; vertex, face, edge | p.18 |

**Fluency by year end:** the full 10×10 table [p.7]. Mental calculation is stressed throughout [p.9, p.13].

**2. Game fit**
- **Good:** place-value building and splits to 10,000; compare `<>=`; skip-count sequences with a gap; number line placement and "between which thousands?"; +/− with a number pad; missing number on either side; ×/÷ facts (Leitner; D23 arena); arrays; `×10 ×100`; remainder (share with leftovers); divisibility by 2/5/10 (sort); rounding (number line snap); order of operations (pick the result); angle type (pick); triangle classification (sort into a 2×3 grid); rectangle area by counting or ×; read a clock or 24-hour digital time and match; durations; read a bar chart or pictogram.
- **Poor:** open problems ("find all solutions", 18-matchstick polygons), problem posing, box nets by folding, drawing rectangles freehand. Two of these can be done as a game instead: a grid tap-to-paint can replace freehand drawing, and picking the right net from three can replace folding.

**3. Misconceptions named (useful as distractors)**
- Judging angle size by **arm length** [p.14]. Angles shown in only one orientation [p.13].
- The remainder notation `17:7 = 2(3)` and `13:5 = 2(3)` suggests the two are equal [p.9].
- "**Greater by**" vs. "**greater times**", for multiplicative comparison [p.8, p.13].
- "Just add a 0" when multiplying by 10 [p.11].
- Equal area does not mean equal perimeter [p.15].
- The `=` sign read as "the answer comes next" (hence equations with the sign on the left) [p.6–7].
- Whether a remainder can be 3 when dividing by 2 [p.10].

**4. Representations:** number line (fill in, exact or approximate placement, which tens/hundreds a number lies between), place-value house, abacus, tables [p.3]. Arrays, equal jumps on a number line, patterns on the hundred chart [p.7]. Area rectangles for the distributive law [p.10–11]. Number lines for rounding [p.13]. Grids, geoboards, tangram and pattern blocks for area [p.15]. Gematria comes up as a link [p.3, p.5].

**Fluency:** mental × of round numbers [p.4]. Mental ÷ by a 1-digit divisor or whole tens [p.7].

**2. Game fit**
- **Good:** place value and comparing to a million; rounding; number line with big ticks; column +/− (digit-by-digit pad); 2-digit × 1-digit through an area model; divisibility 3/6/9 (sort); order of operations (pick the step to do first, then the result); **fractions**: name the shaded part, build a fraction on strips, place it on the 0–1 line, compare with `<>=`, find a fraction of a quantity (drag counters), add like fractions on strips; parallel/perpendicular (tap pairs); match a quadrilateral to its properties, "which has two pairs of parallel sides?"; count diagonals; unit conversion (pick); a reasonable-estimate pick (door is 200 cm); calendar questions; read a pie or bar chart.
- **Poor:** long division as a full algorithm (better done step by step or as estimation), constructions with a ruler, "which one doesn't belong — explain", inclusion reasoning, data projects.

**3. Misconceptions named**
- "**1/3 < 1/4 because 3 < 4**" [p.12].
- Half of different wholes is different: half of 10 apples ≠ half of 20 [p.13].
- Non-identical or discrete wholes [p.13].
- Quadrilaterals seen only in prototype orientation [p.18].
- 0 behaves differently in + and × [p.6].
- Last-digit checks reveal errors (76 × 5 ≠ 456) [p.6].
- Converting between non-adjacent units (km → cm) is out of scope [p.19].
- A bar-chart axis that doesn't start at 0 misleads [p.21].
- Decimal-structure "rules" taught without understanding [p.4].

**4. Representations:** number line, place-value house, tables [p.2]. Rectangle area model for × [p.5]. Splitting the dividend into addends, short and long division [p.8]. **Fraction strips, "cake" (circle) model, rectangle model** [p.14]. Continuous and discrete wholes [p.13]. Dot and square grids [p.19]. Pie chart linked to part-of-whole [p.21].

### Hebrew (1st grade guidance, 2025–26)

- **Vowel order is not fixed nationally** ("varies between reading methods"). Ministry practice boards group:
  kamatz/patach/chataf-patach/shva → chirik → cholam → shuruk/kubutz → segol/tsere. Default to this; make it configurable.
- Required: all letters and vowel marks; אהו"י letters; same-sound letters ט/ת, ח/כ, ק/כ, ב/ו, ש/ס, א/ה/ע;
  dagesh pairs בּ/ב, כּ/כ, פּ/פ; final letters; writing each letter with correct stroke direction (rounded script).
- Phonological awareness: opening sound (צליל פותח), closing sound (צליל סוגר), segmenting and blending; rhyme and syllables in gan.
- Decoding: letter+vowel units blended into words (פִּי־צָה), then larger chunks by the end of 1st grade.
- Nikud is formally removed during 3rd grade.
- A new language curriculum for gan to 2nd grade is being published this school year — re-check before Stages 2–3.

### Hebrew letters and phonological awareness, gan to 1st grade (read 2026-10-29)

Sources: **[C]** curriculum gan–2nd (תשפ"ו) `…/Yesodi/tohnit-limudim-safa.pdf` · **[G]** required goals by age
`https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Pop/0files/ivrit_hinuch_leshoni/Yesodi/acquisition-reading-writing.pdf` ·
**[H]** 1st-grade guidance `…/Yesodi/horaa.pdf` · **[R]** RAMA 1st-grade reading test, teacher guide
`https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Rama/Yesodot_heb_1_teacher_instructions.pdf` · **[K]** gan letter-knowledge booklet
`https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/PreSchool/alef-bet1.pdf`

- **Ages 3–5** ([G] p.7, 10–11): rhyme; split words into syllables and blend them; opening syllable.
- **Ages 5–6, end of gan** ([G] p.16–19): segment/blend below the syllable (consonant+vowel units like בָּ);
  isolate the opening consonant+vowel; isolate the **closing consonant** (what ends דבש?); opening consonant tied to
  the letter's name and shape; syllable deletion (כפתור without כף → תור). Letter names matched to shapes.
  No required letter count; children arrive in 1st grade knowing ~18 of 22 on average ([C] p.77).
- **Teaching order hints:** full phoneme segmentation is "unnatural" in Hebrew; consonant+vowel is the accessible
  unit ([C] p.72). Start opening-sound work with continuous sounds ש ז ס ל ר מ נ ח ([G] p.18).
- **Names vs sounds:** teach both; a letter's name begins with its sound; activities may use either ([C] p.74, [G] p.18).
- **1st grade** ([G] p.26–31, [H]): no fixed letter order (depends on the school's reading method); all letters,
  finals during the year, same-sound letters ט/ת כ/ח ס/ש ב/ו א/ע/ה כ/ק, and בּ/ב כּ/כ פּ/פ שׁ/שׂ (sort words by
  hard/soft sound, right/left shin). Explicit teaching done within about six months.
- **Handwriting:** both print and cursive are taught in 1st grade, with emphasis on cursive (כתב עגול); letters grouped
  by stroke direction (arcs: ר ה ת ח ק ב; top-down: י ו מ נ ן). **No official stroke-order chart was found** — it lives in textbooks.
- **Digital use** ([C] p.157–160): simple, short, focused; large fonts, wide letter spacing, immediate feedback, no distracting effects.
- **RAMA assessment items to mirror** ([R]): letter names (22 cards; name or sound accepted; common confusions
  כ/ח, ו/ז, ק/כ, ת/ט); opening consonant — hear a sound, pick 1 of 4 pictures; closing consonant — see a picture,
  say the last sound; opening consonant+vowel — hear "סִי", pick סירה among 4 pictures.

### Nikud and reading, 1st–2nd grade (read 2026-10-29)

Sources as above, plus **[F]** ministry reading-fluency test for grades 2–6 (2021)
`https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Mazkirut_Pedagogit/HebrewPrimary/assessment/fluency-assessment-2021.pdf`.

- **Nikud order is explicitly not binding** ("סדר סימני הניקוד אינו מחייב", [G] p.29); most methods start with /a/.
  Practice each sign alone (בָּנָה), then mixed (סִפֵּר). Units are taught as letter+sign (שִׁ) and letter+vowel letter (שִׁי) ([G] p.27).
  Drills: unit lists where only the letter or only the sign changes; matching a word-opening unit to pictures.
- **Vowel letters:** אהו"י as consonants and vowels (ו in ורד vs שוק); final /a/ /e/ written as sign + ה (שָׁנָה);
  contrast פר/פרה ([G] p.27–28). No grade 1–2 guidance on shva na/nach was found.
- **Decoding path (grade 1):** unit → word (פִּי/צָה → פיצה); words built from units; linear blending first
  (מִ-שְׁ-קֶ-פֶ-ת), larger chunks by end of grade 1. Always check meaning, "not reading for the sake of practice" ([G] p.28–29, [H] p.7, 32).
- **End of grade 1:** all letters, all nikud signs, אהו"י, homophones and homographs ([G] p.26).
- **Grade 2:** more automatic word recognition; prefixes (ו ה ש ב כ ל מ) and endings (ים/ות) read as units; a few
  frequent words without nikud; nikud removed formally only in grade 3 ([G] p.34–35, [H] p.5).
- **Fluency test [F]:** 80 pointed words, score = correct in 45 s; story read aloud with words-per-minute. No national norms.
  *Not used:* timed tasks are excluded for grades ≤ 2 by CLAUDE.md rules.
- **Item formats to mirror ([G], [R]):** match words and sentences to pictures; cloze in sentences (incl. homophones על/אל);
  semantic sorting / odd-one-out; build sentences from cards; order sentences into a story; missing unit beside a picture.
- **Shared and digital reading:** read-aloud and dialogic shared reading are "especially important in grade 1" ([H] p.15);
  books with prominent words in the signs already learned ([G] p.29). Digital: simple closed interface, large font,
  wide spacing; narration with word highlighting and adjustable speed are named as useful; extras only when tied to the plot ([C] p.157–160).

### English, elementary — detail (read 2026-11-02)

Sources: Curriculum 2020 (Table 4 p.19, Table 5 p.20, outcomes pp.23–36), Pre-Foundation guide (2009, pp.6–15) and Pre-A1
can-dos (2019), Building Blocks overview (pp.2–4), Lexical Bands (Excel, 2020), Grammar Band I.

- **Levels:** Pre-A1 (Pre-Band I) = grade 3 / early grade 4; A1 Band I Core I = grade 4 → mid 5; Core II = mid 5 → end 6.
  Grades 1–2: no requirement (optional exposure only).
- **First a listening/speaking period** (about a year before grade 4) before reading. Pre-A1 outcomes: recognise taught words and
  chunks by ear, match words to pictures, know letters upper + lower case, read left to right; trace/copy letters and words.
- **Literacy order:** oral language → phonological awareness (syllables, rhyme, first/middle/last sound, blend/segment) →
  alphabetics (letter names, sounds and shapes together, upper and lower case together; single-letter sounds before digraphs) →
  decoding (closed and open syllables first, ≈ 53% of beginners' words; then silent-e, digraphs, vowel teams, word families,
  -s/-ed/-ing) → fluency. Read only words whose correspondences were taught.
- **Vocabulary:** Pre-A1 ≈ 200 receptive/productive (chunks like "What's your name?", basic nouns, verbs, function words);
  A1 cumulative ≈ 1,400 receptive / 1,200 productive. **The Lexical Bands file is "All rights reserved" — do not ship it;**
  write our own list of common words (common English words themselves are not protected).
- **Item formats:** pick the picture for a spoken word; same/different first sound; rhyme; blend onset + rime (/t/ + an → tan);
  letter by name / by sound; match word to picture; complete a frame sentence; sequence story pictures; trace and copy.
- **Hebrew-speaker difficulties:** /b/–/p/, /v/–/w/, /iː/–/ɪ/ (feel/fill) — use minimal pairs; left-to-right reading; letter
  direction. Instructions may be in Hebrew; sounds stay English.

### English (Curriculum 2020, elementary)

- Mandatory from 3rd grade. Pre-A1 (Pre-Foundation) in 3rd and early 4th: listening and speaking first,
  then alphabet knowledge and phonics, reading built sound → letter → fluency.
- A1: Band I Core I (4th to mid-5th), Core II (mid-5th to end of 6th). About 1,200 words by end of 6th.
- Word lists: the ministry's "Lexical Bands" Excel file, linked from the English elementary page. Use it as the word database.
- 10+ encounters are needed to master a word → spaced review (DECISIONS D7).

## Computer science, K–2 (Stage 8a, coding island)

- **Ministry, kindergarten "חשיבה מחשובית" (computational thinking):**
  - Skills: sequences of actions, pattern recognition, planning, spatial orientation, problem solving.
  - Method: physical games in the gan plus screen environments such as ScratchJr.
  - Sources: pop.education.gov.il/kindergarten/topics/computational-thinking; the guide "הייטק בגנים" (meyda.education.gov.il/files/PreSchool/hitech/ogdan_ganim.pdf).
- **Ministry, elementary "חשיבה מחשובית ורובוטיקה":** grades 3–6, the basis for Stage 8b (pop.education.gov.il → computer-science-software-engineering-cyber/elementary).
- **CSTA K–12 standards, level 1A (K–2):**
  - model daily processes as algorithms (AP-08);
  - decompose a problem into a precise sequence (AP-11);
  - debug sequences and simple loops;
  - create programs with visual instructions, without text.
- **Level 1B (3–5)** adds conditionals, operators and comparing algorithms (for 8b).
- **Prior art, patterns only:**
  - picture books (*Hello Ruby*; *First Steps in Coding: What's an Algorithm?*): an algorithm is a recipe, and a bug is a step you fix;
  - Bee-Bot, Cubetto and CodeMonkey Jr: absolute arrow cards on a grid → run → watch → fix → repeat.
  - Research on Bee-Bot with 5–6 year olds reports gains in computational thinking.

## Best practices (research-backed)

- Hirsh-Pasek et al. 2015 ("four pillars"): active (thinking, not just tapping), engaged without distracting
  extras, meaningful (real-life context), socially interactive. `https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/educational-apps.html`
- Mastery-based progression; spaced repetition (Leitner); immediate informative feedback; adaptive help;
  short sessions for young children; spoken instructions with replay for pre-readers; multi-sensory (trace + hear + say).

## Technical notes

- **Hebrew TTS:** iOS has a he-IL voice; Android Chrome may list he-IL yet fall back to English; desktop Chrome
  often has none (Windows has "Asaf"). Isolated letter sounds and syllables are unreliable → record them (DECISIONS D3).
  Offline neural options for pre-generating: Phonikud, HebTTS.
- **Letter tracing:** no open Hebrew stroke-order dataset exists; author SVG stroke paths for 27 forms and check
  direction plus rough path with generous tolerance. English printable tracing SVGs: `github.com/domsson/tracedletters`.
- **Fonts (OFL):** Rubik (UI, bundled via `@fontsource-variable/rubik`); Noto Sans Hebrew or Alef for text with nikud
  (most reliable mark placement — verify visually); Playpen Sans Hebrew for handwriting practice.
